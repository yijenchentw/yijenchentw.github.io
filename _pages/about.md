My research focuses on phonology and phonetics, particularly tone and tone sandhi in Chinese languages. I investigate the universal and language-specific phonetic, phonological, and prosodic constraints underlying tonal variation across languages. Using empirical evidence, I aim to test and refine phonological theories and advance our understanding of the principles governing tonal patterns.

I received my Ph.D. from the Graduate Institute of Linguistics at National Chengchi University. My dissertation, *Generalized Directionality in Shandong Dialects*, examines the application of tone sandhi in trisyllabic words and phrases across four Shandong dialects.

Currently, I am an adjunct lecturer in the English Department of National Chengchi University, where I teach *Articulatory Phonetics*. Previously, I worked as a postdoctoral research fellow at the Research Center for Translation, Compilation and Language Education, National Academy for Educational Research.
